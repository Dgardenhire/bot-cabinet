# Decision frame: Freelance photography vs. staying through the supervisor course

## Situation

Alex works full time as a warehouse shift lead, has a small savings buffer, and a two-year-old at home. On weekends Alex does photography for friends and enjoys it. Alex is weighing going freelance as a photographer against staying in the current role, and is also considering a supervisor course at work that starts next month.

## Direction

Alex wants to keep options open — not to waste the supervisor course, not to stay forever, and not to take a pay cut this year. The direction is to test whether freelance photography can become a viable path without closing off the supervisor route or putting finances at risk.

## Constraints

- Time: Three evenings a week available, plus weekends; the supervisor course starts next month and will take up scheduled time.
- Energy: Parenting a two-year-old on top of full-time work; weekend photography already has a real time cost.
- Money: No pay cut this year; small savings buffer limits how soon the freelance income needs to support the household.
- Obligations: Family time with a two-year-old, existing full-time work, and the supervisor course commitment.

## The decision

Should I commit to the supervisor course and stay in my current role for now, or try a low-cost test of freelance photography this year? (by end of the year)

## Ideas, plans, commitments

- Idea: Freelance photography as a side career.
- Plan: Take the supervisor course next month, keep the current job, and evaluate photography income as a side path.
- Commitment (confirmed): No pay cut this year; keep the full-time job until freelance income is stable enough to cover current pay.

## Options

| Option | Benefit | Cost | Fits constraints? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Take the supervisor course and stay in the current role | Keeps income, advances a career path, uses the course without wasting it | Delays freelance work; limited time for photography | Yes — matches the no-pay-cut and course commitments |
| Test freelance photography on evenings/weekends while taking the course | Low-cost trial of the real goal; keeps the supervisor path open | Extra time burden; income may not be reliable yet | Yes, only if the test is small and reversible |
| Drop the supervisor course and go freelance immediately | Pursues the photographer dream fastest | Risk of income loss, no safety net this year, course options lost | No — violates the no-pay-cut constraint |

## Next test

Run a one-evening-per-week freelance photography test for four weeks, by the end of the month, to learn whether there is steady demand and whether the income shows up without damaging family time or the course schedule.

## Open questions

- What does the supervisor course actually cover, and is staying in that career route a firm preference or just a fallback?
- How much time can the freelance test realistically take without cutting into parenting a two-year-old?
- What is a realistic baseline month for freelance income, and how much buffer would make that week acceptable?
