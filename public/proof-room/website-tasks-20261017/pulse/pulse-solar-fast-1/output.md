Baseline: 100 unique visitors → 10 click setup → 2 recorded first result.

Missing evidence: no repeat-use event exists, so there is no measure of whether a visitor came back, used the result a second time, or achieved a second outcome. There is no count of first-result users who repeated. There is no measurement of each setup click's outcome (for example, whether the 2 first results happened within the same session or across visits). Counts are too small to support causation and not randomized.

Experiments (approved states, only if you authorize them):
1. Change the setup flow to require a confirmation step before the first result is considered complete, and measure the first-result rate per qualified click. Success rule: first-result rate improves while repeat-use remains unmeasured. Stop rule: first-result rate does not move after 50 qualified clicks.
2. Add a short onboarding nudge that asks first-result users one optional follow-up question, and measure whether the first-result count changes. Success rule: first-result count is unchanged or higher. Stop rule: first-result count drops below baseline after 20 eligible users. Both stop rules act on counts alone and do not assert that a drop equals a change in outcomes.

I have not changed tracking, spent money, or claimed causality. These are proposals only.
