## Venture brief (hypothesis only)

- **Idea:** Subscription newsletter for tutors at $10/month.
- **Current status:** Unvalidated hypothesis.
- **Known facts:** 3 tutors tried a free mock-up; 1 asked about price; 0 paid; no market-size evidence.
- **Not known / not proven:** content value, target segment, frequency, acquisition path, conversion, retention, market size, profitability.
- **Forecast status:** Any revenue or demand estimate is speculative. The 3 mock-up trials are usage evidence, not payment evidence.

## One no-spend demand test (proposed, not executed)

**Test name:** Paid-intent check with the 3 tutors who already tried the free mock-up.

**Method (no spend, no build, no publish):**
- If separately approved for contact, ask each of the 3 tutors the same structured questions:
  1. “If this newsletter were $10/month, would you subscribe now?”
  2. “What specific part would make it worth paying for?”
  3. “What would stop you from paying?”
  4. “If launched at $10/month, may I record your ‘yes, charge me when available’ as non-binding intent?”
- Capture verbatim responses and objections.
- Do not collect payment, create accounts, or promise launch.

**Rules set before results:**

- **Success (continue to a slightly larger test, not a commitment):** At least 2 of 3 give a specific, reason-based “yes” and identify concrete value they would pay for; price is not the dominant objection.
- **Revise:** 1 of 3 shows intent, or feedback shows a fixable mismatch in content, frequency, audience, or price framing; revise the offer and re-test with the same or next small group.
- **Stop:** 0 of 3 show intent; “free only” is the dominant response; price/value is rejected outright; or the value proposition cannot be stated clearly after feedback.

**What this result would prove:** Whether price is immediately rejected by some existing trial users and what objections exist.

**What this result would not prove:** Actual payment, retention, market size, scalability, profitability, demand beyond these 3 tutors, or willingness under real billing. Market-size evidence remains missing and must be addressed separately.
