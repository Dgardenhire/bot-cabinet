**No-spend demand test: “pre-pay pledge” landing page test**

Setup (no tools, no contacts):
1. Draft a simple one-page landing page describing the newsletter: name, format, frequency, content, and the $10/month price.
2. Include a single, specific call-to-action: “Reserve a spot for $10/month” or “Join the waitlist for $10/month.”
3. Present it only to people who show pre-existing interest in the exact newsletter (e.g., tutors who already asked about pricing, or tutors who signed up for the mock-up). Do not broadcast or cold-contact.
4. Track: how many visit the page, how many express interest, how many pre-pay/pledge $10, how many drop off.

Rules (set before results arrive):
- **Success**: at least 10 visitors express interest AND at least 3 of those pre-pay $10, OR at least 2 visitors pre-pay $10 with 5+ total visitors. This is a small, deliberate bar — a meaningful vote of willingness to pay among the small interested pool.
- **Revise**: pre-pay count is 0–2 while interest is under 10, or interest is under 5 and pre-pay is 0. In this case, change one variable at a time (e.g., price wording, monthly subscription, format, or audience framing), then re-run the same test with the same rules. No spend on ads or outreach.
- **Stop**: no visitors express interest after 1 full test cycle, or after two consecutive revise cycles show no pre-pay conversion. The test is negative; do not proceed to paid outreach or a paid product without new evidence.

**Key caveat from the supplied material**
The free mock-up used 3 tutors, one of whom asked about price; none paid. Three data points are far too small to support a market-size claim, and the test is designed to generate *some* evidence rather than to prove demand. The rules above are deliberately conservative and would refuse to treat interest or traffic as proof of demand.
