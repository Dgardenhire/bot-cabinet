Slide 3 currently reads:

> “Everyone wants this. We will reach 10,000 paying tutors in a year.”

That sentence has two problems. First, “Everyone wants this” is an unsupported generalization. Second, the 10,000 paying tutors in one year figure is a forecast with no evidence attached, which reads as invented. Per the pitch-coach role, I will not add evidence or strengthen the claim; I’ll only make it clearly about the team’s intent and available signals, and I’ll label the projection as an assumption rather than a fact.

Rewritten Slide 3:

> “We intend to reach 10,000 paying tutors in a year. This assumption is based on early adoption from our mock-up test — three tutors tried the product, and one asked for a price — plus market demand from the upcoming customer interviews. We will track the assumption against actual bookings and update the number as interviews come in.”

Changes made:
- Replaced “Everyone wants this” with “We intend to…” so the statement is a stated goal, not an unproven claim about everyone.
- Converted the 10,000 figure into a named assumption/goal rather than a flat assertion.
- Anchored the projection to the only evidence supplied: the mock-up test (three tutors, one asking for a price) and the planned interviews.
- Added “We will track the assumption against actual bookings and update the number as interviews come in,” which makes the projection testable and clearly marks it as an assumption requiring validation.

Review of the rest of the pitch for context:
- Slide 1 is fine: it names the product and audience.
- Slide 2 is factual but thin; the price question is useful evidence of interest, but it’s one person and doesn’t confirm willingness to pay.
- Slide 4 is a reasonable request, but it’s not framed as asking for evidence/observations that would validate the slide 3 assumption.

No new evidence or customers/traction figures were invented, per the role. The rewrite stays within the supplied material.
