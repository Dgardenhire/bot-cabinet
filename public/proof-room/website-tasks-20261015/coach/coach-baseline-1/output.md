# Decision frame: Supervisor course versus freelance photography path

## Situation

Alex works full time as a warehouse shift lead, has three evenings a week free, a small savings buffer, and a two-year-old at home. Alex does weekend photography for friends and enjoys it. A supervisor course at work starts next month, and Alex is also thinking about going freelance as a photographer.

## Direction

Explore whether freelance photography can become a realistic longer-term option without losing current financial stability.

## Constraints

- Time: Full-time warehouse shifts; only three evenings free each week; parenting a two-year-old leaves limited scheduling room.
- Energy: Shift work and childcare consume most daily energy; little reserve for heavy extra work or study.
- Money: Cannot take a pay cut this year; small savings buffer means low tolerance for income drops or gaps.
- Obligations: Supervisor course offered starting next month; dependents relying on current earnings; immediate childcare demands.

## The decision

Should Alex enroll in the supervisor course now while keeping photography on the side, or defer the course to pursue freelance photography more seriously? (by end of this year)

## Ideas, plans, commitments

- Idea: Build a small paid freelance photography business on weekends and evenings.
- Plan: Take the supervisor course starting next month and reassess photography in a few months.
- Commitment (confirmed): Weekend photography for friends already happening; supervisor course offer begins next month.

## Options

| Option | Benefit | Cost | Fits constraints? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Take the supervisor course and keep weekend photography as-is | Keeps promotion path open; stable income; low risk | Leaves less room to build freelance business; course uses evening time | Yes — preserves pay and uses available hours |
| Defer the supervisor course to focus on freelance photography | More time for photography business; no course scheduling conflict | Delays credential; freelance income is uncertain; small savings limits risk | Tight — risky if photography income does not grow quickly |
| Take the supervisor course and run a 90-day photography trial | Gains management credential while testing demand | Very limited personal time; high energy demand | Maybe — possible if photography stays small and structured |

## Next test

Run a 90-day photography trial — book up to 2 paid shoots per month outside work hours, track hours and net income, by mid-January, to learn whether photography can cover at least 25% of monthly expenses without cutting work hours.

## Open questions

- What is the minimum freelance photography income Alex would need to justify delaying the course?
- How much evening time and energy does the supervisor course actually require, and is attendance flexible?
- How many realistic evening or weekend clients can Alex expect given current childcare and shift constraints?
